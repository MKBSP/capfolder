Try a cap table app before you type in yours

Capfolder 0.1.3: a sample company, three quick questions, and a checklist that ticks itself

A new cap table tool opens empty. To find out whether you trust it, you have to type in your real company first: share counts, founders, every SAFE. That's a lot of sensitive numbers for an app you met five minutes ago.

This week's release lets you look first.

A sample company. Pick "Try the sample company" and Capfolder opens Acme Inc.: 2 founders, 2 employees with options, an option pool and 3 SAFEs. Click any row to see how its vesting is worked out. Nothing you do is saved.

It even has 11 gaps in "What's missing" on purpose: the signed documents a lawyer would ask for in due diligence. Better to see that list on fake data first.

Three quick questions. SAFEs? Option pool? Priced round? The setup steps that don't apply disappear.

A Get started list. Seven steps that tick themselves from what's in your cap table, each with the button that does it.

A one-minute tour with six stops, and a "?" next to valuation cap, discount, cliff, par value and vesting start.

What it doesn't do yet: the sample is read-only, so you can't try e-signing in it.

Capfolder is free, open source, and keeps your cap table in a folder on your own computer. The full post, with every screenshot.
