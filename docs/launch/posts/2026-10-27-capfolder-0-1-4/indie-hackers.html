Answer the question your users actually ask

I'm building Capfolder, an open-source cap table app. A cap table answers "who owns what?". It took me a while to notice that founders and employees almost never ask that. They ask "how much of mine has vested, and when is the rest mine?"

Until this release, Capfolder answered that one holding at a time. You clicked into a row and read the math. Correct, but slow, and nobody does it.

What we did

Version 0.1.4 puts the time question first:

  1. A Vesting screen. The option pool at a glance (total, granted, vested, available), and a row per grant that opens to its graph: the cliff, the monthly steps, today, and where vesting stops if someone leaves.
  2. A person page you can read in five seconds. Vested, next vest and fully vested sit next to the name. The graph is on the left, the holding and its history on the right.
  3. An import check. Coming from Pulley, Carta or Cake? Type in the old tool's totals and Capfolder checks them to the share.
  4. "What needs my attention?" Up to 3 things to do next, each with a Take me there button.

The lesson: put the answer where the question starts

The numbers on the new screens aren't new. The engine already worked out every vest, to the share. What changed is where they show up: next to the name, before you click anything.

Two things I'd do again:

  • One engine, many screens. The Vesting screen, the person page and the Excel export all draw from the same vesting math. A screen can't drift from the others, because none of them does its own math.
  • Ship the useful part without the fancy part. "What needs my attention?" sounds like an AI feature. It isn't: it's a short list built from the app's own list of missing documents and open signatures. It works with no AI connected, which is how most people will first meet it.

What I don't know yet

Whether people find the Vesting tab without being told. Your cap table never leaves your computer, so I can't watch: I'll learn from people telling me. And the import check compares totals, not every row, so you still check a few people by hand.
