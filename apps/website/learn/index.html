Startup equity, explained

  • What is a cap table?

    A cap table (capitalization table) lists who owns your startup: shares, options, SAFEs and the option pool. What goes in it, a worked example and common mistakes.

  • What is a SAFE?

    A SAFE (simple agreement for future equity) lets a startup raise money now and issue shares later. Post-money SAFEs, valuation caps, discounts and a worked example.

  • Fully diluted shares

    Fully diluted shares count every share that exists or could exist: options, the whole option pool, warrants. How to calculate it, an example and common mistakes.

  • Pre-money vs post-money valuation

    Pre-money is what your company is worth before new money comes in; post-money is after. The formula, a worked seed round example and the mistakes founders make.

  • SAFE valuation cap and discount

    How a SAFE's valuation cap and discount decide how many shares the investor gets. The math, a worked example with three SAFEs, and which one wins.

  • Option pool and ESOP

    An option pool (often called an ESOP) is the block of shares a startup sets aside for employee options. How it works, how big it should be, and a worked example.

  • Vesting schedules and cliffs

    How a 4-year vesting schedule with a 1-year cliff works, what happens when someone leaves, acceleration, and a month-by-month example for an employee and founders.

  • The 83(b) election

    An 83(b) election lets you pay tax on restricted stock when you receive it instead of as it vests. Who should file, the 30-day deadline, how to file and an example.

  • What is a 409A valuation?

    A 409A valuation is an independent appraisal of your common stock, used to set option strike prices. When you need one, how long it lasts, and what goes wrong without it.

  • Share dilution

    Share dilution is the drop in your ownership when a company issues new shares. How it works, a worked example through SAFEs and a seed round, and how to plan.

  • ISO vs NSO stock options

    ISOs and NSOs differ in who can get them and how they are taxed. The ISO rules, the $100k limit, the 3-month rule after leaving, and a worked example.

  • Liquidation preference

    A liquidation preference decides who gets paid first when a startup is sold. 1x non-participating, participating preferred, seniority and a worked exit example.
