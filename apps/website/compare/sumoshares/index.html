Capfolder vs SumoShares

Both are free, open-source Carta alternatives. SumoShares is a web app you deploy to your own Cloudflare account. Capfolder is a desktop app: no setup, and your cap table is a folder on your computer.

In short: leaving Carta with lots of option holders and a developer to run it? SumoShares has the holder portal and online exercise. An early-stage founder with SAFEs, signatures to collect and no developer? Capfolder.

This comparison is written by the Capfolder team. Checked October 2026.

Pros and cons

SumoShares does well

  • Employees log in and see their equity, and exercise options online.
  • 409A and option-deadline reminders by email, every day.
  • A careful Carta import that checks every holder against Carta's numbers.
  • Free, MIT licensed, cheap to run.

Where SumoShares falls short

  • You need a developer to set it up, update it and keep it running.
  • No SAFEs or convertible notes, so it doesn't fit a pre-seed or seed company.
  • No signatures: documents are signed elsewhere and uploaded.
  • New (October 2026), US-only, and no automated tests yet.

Capfolder does well

  • No setup: download, open, done. Works offline.
  • SAFEs, option grants and signatures in one place.
  • Drop in your old documents and they're sorted; missing ones are flagged.
  • Every number shows its math, and the math is tested.

Where Capfolder falls short

  • No employee logins or online exercise yet.
  • No 409A tracking or reminders yet.
  • One computer at a time until sync ships in Pro.

Switching from SumoShares

  1. In SumoShares, open Reports and download ocf.json and the CSVs.
  2. In Capfolder, create your company folder and choose Import, then pick the OCF file.
  3. If the OCF file doesn't import, use the holders and securities CSVs with the spreadsheet import, and tell us.
  4. Drop in your documents. Capfolder sorts them and lists what's missing.
